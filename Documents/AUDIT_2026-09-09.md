**HATI adversarial audit — 9 September 2026**

**Release update, 10 September:** the findings below describe the immutable audited baseline. The [v2.5 release assessment](V25_RELEASE.md) records the implemented experimental replacement, further fixes and remaining research gaps. Statements below that the replacement is proposed describe the audit-time state.

Audited baseline: `0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486`, branch `feat/v2.0-heatmap`. Code links below deliberately refer to that immutable baseline, before the accompanying fixes. This report distinguishes reproduced defects, scientific limitations, and hypotheses requiring the Linux products. No ISIS application or ingestion was run on the Windows machine. The real eight-frame manifest, cubes, geometry sidecars and closure records are absent here; the reported eight-frame results have not been independently rerun. The NOBILE03 DEM, orthoimages and labels *are* present and were inspected.

**Verdict.** The shadow physics is usable; the current evidence is not a validated hazard detector. The strongest result in the audit is a counterexample: a completely stationary albedo stripe passes the claimed solar-convergence test under the stated 88-degree observing geometry. Changing the segmentation front end will not repair that inference. Keep ingestion and physical geometry, replace the decision statistic with a joint image-domain comparison against stationary albedo and terrain. Do not describe the present map as clearance or the current synthetic recovery table as field performance.

**1. The stationary-stripe counterexample and what the null actually tests**

I passed eight identical 512×512 arrays to the real detector: background 120, a nine-pixel vertical stripe of intensity 20, Sun elevations 4 degrees, map azimuths evenly spaced from −44 to +44 degrees. No moving shadow or relief was present. The per-frame casts were `[0,0,1,1,1,1,0,0]`. Four frames selected the same stripe endpoint; dilation radius 3 produced 29 pixels with four votes. The original spatial-shift procedure, with seed 11 and 200 trials, produced mean zero and zero exceedances. This is an executable counterexample, not a speculation about the real survivors. Its detection part is retained in `tests/test_audit_regressions.py` as an explicitly documented limitation.

The reason is selection on orientation. Frames that would contradict the stripe are simply allowed to abstain. Four favourable angles suffice. The filter checks projected extents and the up-Sun extreme; it never establishes that image darkness moved as predicted. See [voting and accumulation](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/shadow_kinematics_real.py#L235) and [the null/reporting logic](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/shadow_kinematics_real.py#L878).

The spatial null asks whether independently displaced vote maps coincide less than registered maps. Persistent albedo, crater corners, topographic shading and real boulders all have spatial correspondence. Destroying it removes the main nuisance that the scientific null must preserve. A tiny tail frequency under this control says nothing about which of those explanations is correct.

There is also a concrete edge bias. The old null discards shifted votes outside the image. For uniform shifts in ±W/4 and uniform feature positions, average one-axis retention is 7/8 and two-axis retention is 49/64≈0.766. This is an illustrative homogeneous calculation, not a measured correction factor for this site. Cropping and nonstationary terrain further alter coincidence counts. The old rule also maps all small positive *and negative* offsets to +40, creating an artificial probability mass. The patch rolls completed vote maps to preserve mass and samples symmetric offsets, but toroidal seams and nonstationarity remain. It is now explicitly a correspondence diagnostic.

The primary null must instead be “the registered scene is explained by stationary albedo plus resolved terrain, photometric variation and registration uncertainty, with no additional compact relief object.” It must allow ordinary cast shadows from the resolved terrain. A null containing only independent noise or a flat surface is unacceptable.

The raw k-of-N count is defensible as a discovery statistic, provided one frame contributes at most once and observable opportunities are defined. It is not a probability. Eight frames are not eight equally informative independent trials; clustered azimuths, shared camera geometry and masks reduce information. Agreement discs also merge nearby objects, and connected-pixel area is not an object count. The comment that a fixed agreement fraction necessarily gets “harder” as N grows is too broad: power depends on the true detection probability relative to that fraction and on dependence.

With B=200, zero exceedances should not be reported as p=0; the usual finite-sampling adjustment is `(b+1)/(B+1)`, which gives 1/201 before multiplicity adjustment. Searching k and reporting the successful value adds selection. The patch adjusts for the N−1 k thresholds, but does not correct the entire historical search over window size, width caps, background cuts and radius. More fundamentally, finite-sample corrections do not confer validity on nonexchangeable shifts. See [Phipson and Smyth](https://gksmyth.github.io/pubs/PermPValuesPreprint.pdf).

**2. The counterfactual was allowed to use post-landing data**

The [saved plan](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/data/sweep/plan.json#L39) includes M1496181900LE from March 9, 2025, and several other March 8–14 alternates. Calling the original selector with 24 bins, elevations 1–9 degrees, target 4 degrees and emission cap 40 selects M1496097780LE, acquired March 8, as a primary. The [manifest writer](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/ingest_sweep.py#L1166) then discards UTC entirely. Therefore the code could not certify the central pre-landing claim. Whether the reported eight frames actually included these products remains unknown without their manifest.

The new default excludes everything acquired on or after `2025-03-06T00:00:00Z`, conservatively excluding the whole landing day. Missing dates also fail. A different cutoff is explicit. UTC and cutoff survive into the manifest and are independently checked by science. Acquisition time is not sufficient to establish historical *availability*: record archive release dates, source product versions, DEM availability and kernel/calibration versions for a strict operational counterfactual.

The local DEM and ortho PDS3 labels give product creation in November 2024 and source imaging in August 2012; their PDS4 label was modified in December 2025. A later label migration does not by itself prove the pixels were unavailable before landing. Preserve the distinction. Also distinguish “could have flagged the touchdown neighbourhood” from “would have prevented the accident.” The [LROC account](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/1408) describes faster-than-intended surface contact and a final position inside a 20 m crater. A retrospective image study does not isolate accident causality or reconstruct a usable flight decision.

**3. Geometry: the suspected rotation is right; the error budget is not**

For the configured spherical south-polar projection, with central meridian λ₀=0,

`x = 2R tan(π/4 + φ/2) sin λ`, `y = 2R tan(π/4 + φ/2) cos λ`.

Projected north is `(sin λ, cos λ)` and east is `(cos λ, −sin λ)`. A ground bearing A therefore gives raster direction `(dr,dc)=(-cos(A+λ),sin(A+λ))`. The implemented addition is correct for a north-up raster with those CRS parameters. It is not a generic rule for a changed central meridian, rotated affine transform, another pole or another longitude convention. A new offline test independently differentiates the GDAL/PROJ projection to verify the sign.

ISIS is not limited to image-plane Sun azimuths: `campt` supplies `SubSolarGroundAzimuth`, with the ground bearing convention the code wants. Its universal coordinates are planetocentric latitude and positive-east longitude. Compare the custom bearing directly against that value in saved labels rather than regarding all ISIS azimuth conventions as unknowable. See [campt documentation](https://isis.astrogeology.usgs.gov/9.0.0/Application/presentation/Tabbed/campt/campt.html). Use the local solar vector and explicitly distinguish ellipsoid incidence from local surface incidence when adding a DEM.

At this site polar-stereographic scale is approximately `k=2/(1−sin φ)=1.00207`. Thus 0.9 projected metres corresponds to roughly 0.898 ground metres locally. This 0.2% correction is real but negligible beside slope, PSF and registration uncertainty. The Moon subtends a small angle across 2.16 km; per-frame central geometry is a useful first approximation, but spatial variation and line-scan timing should be propagated in the final forward model.

The reference IMG's attached PDS3 label and GDAL give corner transform `(75558.90, 142717.10)` at 0.9 m/px. The hard-coded values `(75559.35,142716.65)` were the first pixel's *centre*. [Old output code](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/shadow_kinematics_real.py#L1088) used these as corners, displacing GeoTIFF centres by (+0.45,−0.45) m. Its CSV used the same numbers as point coordinates, so those CSV positions could accidentally be correct while disagreeing with raster centres. The patch derives both from the reference transform. The PDS4 XML's negative x and tiny “projection scale factor” are inconsistent with the attached PDS3 georeferencing—the latter matches GDAL. Do not generalize the existing absolute-value workaround into a coordinate convention.

**4. Height is not identified by the present length estimate**

Let L be horizontal distance down-Sun from the vertical projection of a caster top to the shadow termination, and let the receiving terrain rise by Δz(L) along that path. Then

`h = L tan(e) + Δz(L)`.

For a receiving plane rising down-Sun at angle β, `h=L[tan(e)+tan(β)]`. The old estimate drops the second term. At e=4°, β=+1° makes the flat-ground estimate about 20% low; β=−1° makes it about 33% high. Expressed relative to L tan(e), the omitted term is about ±25%. Near grazing illumination this is first-order physics, not a refinement.

The observed darkest component's maximum-minus-minimum projection is neither a PSF-corrected termination nor the vertical projection of the highest point. Threshold erosion, caster radius, segmentation truncation and local slope change the measured length. If the shadow end merges, a root can support existence, but usually gives only a lower bound on length/height. Do not report a precise height from a censored end.

Finite Sun size also matters at low elevation. For a solar angular diameter about 0.53°, a flat-plane first-order estimate of longitudinal penumbra width is `h*csc²(e)*Δe`. At h=0.3 m and e=4° this is about 0.57 m. A fixed isotropic Gaussian in map pixels is not a complete model of that edge, detector footprint, motion smear, off-nadir sampling and two resampling operations. Native detector GSD and effective PSF vary by frame; 0.9 m output posting does not create 0.9 m information.

Useful uncertainty propagation starts with `σ_h² ≈ tan²(e)σ_L² + L²sec⁴(e)σ_e² + σ_Δz²`, with covariance terms retained when errors share the same DEM or alignment. The current average of per-frame maximum-stamped heights is not such an estimator. Heights are selection-biased, nearby regions can supply different maxima, and a histogram of detection pixels weights objects by their agreement-disc area.

**5. Registration and camera processing**

The [original bounded correlator](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/ingest_sweep.py#L626) constructs `ifft(F*conj(G))`, whose peak displacement is already the shift to apply. It negates that displacement, shifts the moving image the wrong way, then relies on unconstrained phase correlation to recover. In a probe with planted displacement (8,−5), the coarse step was (8,−5), although the correction is (−8,5). Fine refinement happened to rescue the answer. Another probe returned approximately (−60,−0.1) under a 20 px cap and reported `at_edge=False`. The patch fixes the sign and rejects refinement leaving the local peak or total bound.

The closure sign itself is correct. But closure is not independent ground truth. Suppose every image contains a dominant pattern `F(x−t_i)` caused by illumination, and the estimator registers that pattern. Direct pair shifts equal `t_i−t_j`, so all cycles close even if every terrain correspondence is wrong. Shared systematic distortion can also be invisible. Twenty-eight pairwise numbers are highly dependent because they reuse eight frames. A median over the entire 2 km window does not bound local residuals at 12 candidate positions.

Keep closure as a necessary consistency diagnostic. Add spatially distributed held-out terrain checkpoints, local residual maps, uncertainty ellipses, per-frame coverage and an explicit absolute-accuracy tie. Register photometrically stable terrain, use stereo/bundle adjustment where feasible, and validate away from the features used to fit. Reject unobservable patches rather than increasing a circular vote radius until they agree.

For residual relief Δh relative to the shape model, planimetric error has magnitude approximately `Δh tan(emission)` in the look direction. Between two images it is the vector difference `Δh[tan(E_i)v_i−tan(E_j)v_j]`. At opposite look directions and E=40°, 10 m of residual relief can yield about 16.8 m differential error. A scalar emission cap is not a residual error bound. The relevant Δh is terrain height *relative to the actual shape model*, not necessarily altitude above the reference sphere.

The assertion that `spiceinit web=yes` necessarily means “no DEM” is unverified. ISIS defaults to `shape=SYSTEM`; the actual shape appears in `Kernels/ShapeModel`. Projection onto a spherical map CRS is also distinct from the shape surface used for camera ray intersection. [ISIS documents these options](https://isis.astrogeology.usgs.gov/9.0.0/Application/presentation/Tabbed/spiceinit/spiceinit.html). The patch retains labels; inspect them on the GPU box. A user shape model must contain planetary radii with correct datum/units and coverage; do not feed an elevation-above-datum TIFF directly to `shape=user`.

The [old processing sequence](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/ingest_sweep.py#L589) skipped echo correction. NAC replicates part of the signal two columns away (one in summed data); this is directly relevant to narrow shadow contrast and roots. The [LROC processing guide](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/data/support/downloads/LROC_NAC_Processing_Guide.pdf) places `lronacecho` after calibration. Added here. Its correction can increase noise, so measure the resulting covariance; a sharper image is not automatically a higher-SNR image.

**6. Injection controls: why the present table cannot validate the detector**

The [positive recovery path](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/shadow_kinematics_real.py#L687) omitted `max_width_px`, falling back to 4, while main used 8 and negative injection passed the configured value. Positive and negative performance did not describe one operating point. Corrected.

The [boulder renderer](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/shadow_kinematics_real.py#L514) only treated widths below 1 px fractionally. A 0.5 m-high object nominally 1.11 px wide, a 1 m-high object nominally 2.22 px wide and a 1.34 m-high object nominally 2.98 px wide all used a single-pixel brush. At 1.35 m the brush suddenly jumped to three pixels; a 2 m object nominally 4.44 px wide used three. Square brushes were also not width-controlled perpendicular to the Sun. Consequently recovery-versus-height mixed geometry with stair-step rendering errors. The replacement integrates a rotated rectangle at 8×8 subpixel samples and retains PSF convolution. This is still a simplified silhouette model, not a validated rock simulator.

Changing that renderer reduced the old favourable half-metre smoke test from its asserted ≥80% recovery to 8%, and another half-metre fixture recovered 0/3. Those two machinery tests now use resolved one-metre objects and pass; this is explicitly documented, not a retained half-metre sensitivity claim. A separate area/orientation test guards the renderer.

Remaining defects in the controls are substantial:

- Craters and ridges still use hard-edged integer rendering with no corresponding PSF/partial coverage. The crater tail starts at the centre rather than explicitly ray tracing the raised rim. These idealized shapes are not a realistic negative class.
- Applying `dn*(1−soft)+darkness*bg*soft` can *brighten* existing dark terrain where `dn < darkness*bg`. At map level this operation is not generally equivalent to shadowing a physical radiance field before optical convolution. Photon/read noise is not regenerated.
- `measured_darkness` takes a global low percentile, not measured interiors of independently identified shadows. It conflates albedo, terrain, noise and saturation, and supplies one value to heterogeneous frames.
- `hits_near` counts any existing candidate near a planted centre as recovery, without baseline subtraction, causal change or one-to-one association. Large crater/ridge tolerances worsen this. Compare before/after detections and assign objects uniquely.
- Default 60 injections yield only 20 negative objects per size. Zero out of 20 has one-sided 95% binomial upper bound `1−0.05^(1/20)≈13.9%`, even before shared terrain dependence. Zero printed percent is not evidence of a negligible field false-positive rate.
- Injections occur after map projection and fitted co-registration. They test the detector on an aligned stack, not EDR-to-result recovery, native sampling, parallax or alignment failure.
- The area cleaner uses SciPy's default four-neighbour connectivity, whereas region extraction uses scikit-image's default eight-neighbour connectivity. An otherwise valid one-pixel diagonal can be removed as isolated pixels before the voter sees it. This is a further orientation-dependent floor, distinct from the already-fixed morphological opening. It remains in the legacy detector and should be eliminated when replacing the segmentation front end.

Use paired injections into fixed sites stratified by native GSD, azimuth information, terrain slope, albedo, shadow occupancy and coverage. Sample subpixel position, object aspect ratio, effective PSF and contrast uncertainty. Inject one object or sufficiently separated objects per trial. Preserve the real baseline and report incremental detections, missed objects and height interval coverage. For a field claim, validate against external annotations, not the same renderer that defines the signal model.

Two statements in the brief also overreach. `0.88^L` assumes independent shadow occupancy along the ray; terrain shadows are correlated, so the measured free-path distribution, conditional on width and orientation, is the relevant quantity. It is not an exact explanation of the ceiling. And a recovered height below the assumed spherical/2h width floor does not logically exclude every real boulder: a low broad object violates that aspect-ratio prior. It does undermine the current *height interpretation*. Likewise, 12 divided by the full 2.16² km² window is 2.57×10⁻⁶ m⁻²; a different density requires an explicitly measured effective area and completeness. Do not infer a population deficit from raw candidates alone.

**7. The replacement I would actually implement**

Use roots to generate candidates, but do not hard-gate on a visibly bright caster. An unresolved caster can have an undetectable bright face; a shadow can begin on dark terrain; a crater rim can create the same local step. A short directional derivative/step filter is useful as a cheap proposal stage. The acceptance decision should fit the full multi-frame photometric evidence.

For candidate ground position b and physical parameters θ=(height, width, shape, receiving-plane slope), ray trace a soft shadow coverage field `S_i(x;b,θ)` using the measured solar vector, resolved DEM/horizon and finite solar disk. For a first controlled experiment, use a rectangle/tapered capsule with length `h/[tan(e_i)+tan(β_i)]` where that planar approximation is valid. Treat width and height separately; do not fix width=2h in the inverse problem. Include a separate lit-object component only when physically justified.

Build an observation operator `P_i` containing native pixel integration, measured PSF, view geometry and map resampling. A simple restricted model is

`H0: y_i = P_i[g_i A(x) + B_i(x)] + ε_i`

`H1: y_i = P_i[(g_i A(x)+B_i(x))(1−a_i S_i(x;b,θ))] + ε_i`.

Here A is a *shared, terrain-locked* albedo field; B includes constrained smooth photometric/resolved-terrain terms; a_i describes bounded shadow contrast. In practice use ray-traced resolved-terrain illumination inside both models rather than expecting a smooth B to explain every crater. Do not give either model unlimited per-frame pixel freedom. Do not fit an arbitrary independent a_i for every frame and then count only successful frames: that recreates the abstention loophole.

For a locally linear, whitened patch model, stack all usable frame pixels into y, nuisance basis X, and shadow template t. With W=C⁻¹ and nuisance residual operator `R=I−X(XᵀWX)⁻¹XᵀW`, a one-sided matched score for a darkening template t is

`Z = −tᵀ W R y / sqrt(tᵀ W R t)`.

Use a pseudoinverse/rank check, not an unstable explicit matrix inverse. This expression assumes a specified template and covariance. Numerically, whiten with a factor of C and project with QR/SVD. If the residualized template norm is nearly zero, shadow and albedo are unidentifiable: emit “insufficient illumination diversity.” A stationary feature should be explained by A; its dark pixels in frames predicting illumination count *against* the relief model.

The full statistic can be `T(b)=max_θ 2[log L1−log L0]`, with physical bounds on contrast, height and width, plus a censored-shadow option. Estimate registration uncertainty independently and marginalize or profile shifts within those measured bounds. Do not let candidate fitting freely realign the same darkness that it is supposed to explain. Group nearly identical illuminations so repetition improves noise precision without pretending to add angular information. Fit on one azimuth group and score predictions on held-out groups; there must be genuinely contrasting illumination among the holdouts.

For merged shadows, only observable incremental attenuation contributes. Pixels already predicted to be in terrain shadow supply little or no information. A short root match can therefore yield evidence without requiring an isolated full streak, but it cannot conjure information from a permanently dark path. Keep “end visible”, “end censored” and “root unobservable” as separate states. Fit full length only when the data constrain it.

**Threshold with a controlled error target.** Decide first whether the target is familywise probability of any false candidate in the search area, false candidates/km², or object-level FDR. For a rigorous initial paper, use map-level familywise control. Fit H0 on independent or cross-fitted background regions, simulate registered stacks under H0 including static albedo, resolved relief, spatially correlated noise, masks and independently measured registration uncertainty, and run the *entire* candidate/template/parameter selection on each simulated map. Calibrate the distribution of the maximum T over all positions, sizes and tested model variants. Use the corresponding upper quantile or Monte Carlo rank `(1+# maxima >= observed)/(B+1)`.

That controls the chosen error only if the null simulation is adequate. Validate the empirical false-alarm budget on withheld real negative regions across sites. A calibration based on independent exchangeable negative blocks can provide a rank guarantee for that sampled domain, but adjacent patches from one polar strip are not automatically exchangeable. With too few independent scenes, say the bound is unvalidated. Neither a nominal Gaussian “5 sigma” cut nor thousands of shifted versions of the same eight frames supplies the missing negative population.

The proposed SNR≈21 is a conditional calculation. For 28% attenuation over 5.4 independent pixels, it requires fractional per-pixel noise around `0.28*sqrt(5.4)/21≈0.031`. Real resampling makes noise correlated; albedo estimation, template mismatch and registration consume information. With covariance C the ideal matched SNR is `sqrt(tᵀC⁻¹t)`, and with nuisance fitting it is smaller. Prove recoverability with the measured covariance and template residual norm before describing it as latent subpixel information. Distinguish subpixel *width*, subpixel *height* and sub-DEM-resolution relief: they are different claims.

**8. DEM channel architecture and the safety product**

The two sensing streams make sense. For safety, retain a common relief/clearance representation while preserving stream-specific evidence and unknowns. A crater and a boulder can both violate clearance; reject a crater as a *boulder label* if needed, but do not discard its hazard evidence. Classification is secondary metadata. Relief sign still matters to footpad support and belly clearance, so collapsing everything into one unqualified scalar also loses necessary mechanics.

The [cross-site test](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/scripts/mare_control_v3.py#L108) labels site membership. It asks whether an arbitrary massif pixel outranks an arbitrary mare pixel. A uniform tilted plane can win that comparison without revealing rocks, and a smooth mare contains dangerous local craters. These AUCs justify a limited site-ranking observation, not a per-object confusion matrix or safety gate. Selection and evaluation on the same two sites do not establish transfer. Effective resolution is also assumed using `max(2*native,COMMON)`; both 2 m and 4 m inputs consequently get the same assumed 8 m width before extra blur, despite the comments invoking a different stereo resolution. This does not measure their MTF or restore aliasing introduced before downsampling.

`terrain_ruggedness_index` always uses immediate neighbours; its `window_px` argument is ignored and it has no physical baseline. On the same planar slope sampled at different postings, its value scales with posting. The new `hati_core` wrapper therefore still cannot honestly promise that both surviving channels are universally transferable physical-baseline measurements. Resample to a verified common effective resolution or define TRI at an explicit physical displacement. RMS slope also includes regional tilt; it is not roughness after detrending. A Gaussian-smoothed slope averaged over 56 m is not a mechanical fit to a lander's feet.

The [core gate](https://github.com/Cyrex567/HATI/blob/0fac80d2561915beffb9d8d04052bd035ca21486/src/hati_core/__init__.py#L95) previously used local smoothed slope for DEM input and windowed RMS slope for channel input. This patch makes those paths agree and excludes nonfinite values. `channels(valid=...)` formerly ignored valid entirely; it now fills only for computation and masks filter support. These are correctness repairs, not validation of the chosen lander threshold. Configuration hashes now include weight values, but still need data, baseline and code identities alongside them.

A landing-site team needs a georeferenced set of candidate touchdown poses, maximum compatible footprint slope, footpad height differences, belly clearance, obstacle/negative-relief intervals, local coverage and uncertainty. Mark “assessed clear for stated limits,” “excluded,” and “unassessed” distinctly. Dilate spatial hazards by the lander contact geometry and navigation uncertainty, not the shadow-vote radius. Integrate over a landing dispersion distribution only after a hazard model is calibrated. A sigmoid or noisy-OR of correlated evidence streams is not a strike probability. Retain source frame IDs and reasons behind every exclusion.

The familiar `1−exp(−λA)` is conditional on a Poisson point-process population and the appropriate collision area. Boulders cluster; craters have extended rims; object size and footprint orientation matter. A mean density alone is insufficient. A deterministic conservative exclusion map with a clearly visible unknown mask is a better interim product than an invented probability surface.

**9. External validation and the result worth publishing**

Start where independent measurements exist, then transfer to the pole. The [Apollo 17 mapped traverse/ortho reference](https://an.rsl.wustl.edu/apollo/map/apMap.aspx?m=17), [USGS Apollo 17 map](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/ckan/dataset/2f509e69-cd20-4638-ab63-618135c776b3/resource/fca2a030-bb4f-48ad-9f61-f0d122a4812b/download/apollo17_15000_rgb_reduced.pdf) and surface photography support manually cross-identified landmarks. They are not an already complete sub-metre height catalogue. Use stereo surface measurements where recoverable and explicitly identify objects with only orbital width labels. Powell et al. describe manual Apollo 17 NAC boulder counts in their [Diviner validation study](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022JE007532); those can inform object selection and independent counts, while Diviner abundance itself is too spatially coarse to validate each small object.

The manually mapped planetary image labels in the [BOULDERING dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/14250874) are another practical source of resolved-object annotations. Separate lunar from Martian images, and retain original image IDs and annotation completeness limits. Using human labels from a machine-learning research dataset does not put a trained model in HATI's decision path. In contrast, automatic global boulder maps should be treated as comparison products, not independent truth. These sources will validate resolved objects first; they do not settle a 0.3 m polar detection claim.

For the crater problem, LROC's [new-crater shapefile](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/SHAPEFILE_GLOBAL_NEW_CRATERS) links before/after imagery. It offers identified crater events, but verify diameter range, polar coverage and completeness before using it. New-crater *absence* in earlier imagery is useful temporal control. The Athena post-landing images can be held out for retrospective crater localization, but must never enter the pre-landing detector or threshold fitting.

The keystone scientific result is a frozen, independently labelled object benchmark reporting recall by physical width/height bin, false detections per assessed km², and confidence intervals, with held-out sites/azimuths and stratification by visibility and native resolution. Include an ablation against a single-frame detector and an albedo-aware model without temporal shadow geometry. Demonstrate that the solar sweep buys information at the same false-alarm budget. Report height bias and interval coverage only for objects whose heights are independently measured; include non-detections and censored lengths.

For the mission-facing claim, the next result is retained landing area versus missed independently labelled hazards under stated lander limits. A per-object confusion matrix alone does not evaluate the footprint decision. A size-frequency distribution comes *after* calibrated completeness, association and area; correcting the current nonmonotonic, near-zero-completeness bins would be unstable. An Athena retrospective is a case study following the independent benchmark, not the benchmark itself.

What is publishable now: the explicit failure mode of endpoint voting under limited illumination, the stationary-albedo counterexample, a properly scoped archival-availability/processing study, and a reproducible synthetic comparison labelled as synthetic. What is not supported: verified subpixel boulder detection, calibrated height accuracy on real objects, a lunar boulder population estimate, “the Sun drives these 12 detections,” clearance certification, or proof HATI would have prevented Athena's failure. The pre-existing papers have older scopes/results; this patch does not regenerate their PDFs or make their numbers current.
